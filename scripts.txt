1. Josh Braun — 4T Framework
Subject: [specific trigger, e.g. "your post on X" / "saw you just launched Y"]
Hey [First Name],
[TRIGGER — one line naming something specific and recent about them: a post, a hire, a launch, a comment.]
[THINK — one line that reframes a problem they likely have, phrased as an observation, not an accusation. e.g. "Most teams doing X end up stuck on Y."]
[THIRD-PARTY CREDIBILITY — one line, a peer result, not your own claim. e.g. "We helped [similar company] fix that in [timeframe] — [specific outcome]."]
[TALK — one low-friction ask. e.g. "Worth a 10-min look?" / "Want me to send the 2-min version?"]
[Your name]

2. Jason Bay — REPLY Method (relevance-first)
Subject: [reference to the specific trigger event]
Hi [First Name],
[Relevance line — the concrete business reason you're reaching out now: a trigger event, role change, funding, hiring spree, public initiative. Must be true and specific, not generic flattery.]
[One sentence connecting that trigger to a problem your offer solves — stated as their likely reality, not a pitch.]
[Optional: one-line proof point, only if you have a directly comparable example.]
[Ask — binary and easy: "Open to a quick chat this week?" or "Mind if I send more info?"]
[Your name]

3. Will Allred / Lavender — Above the Line
Everything that matters must fit in what the recipient reads before deciding to bail — usually the first 1–2 lines on mobile. Write the email backward: draft the ask first, then the proof, then trim the opener until only the sharpest hook survives.
Subject: [4–6 words max, no hype]
[Line 1 — sharpest possible hook. Must earn the next line. No "I hope this finds you well," no company backstory.]
[Line 2 — the one thing that matters: their problem or your proof, whichever is stronger.]
[Optional line 3 — brief context, only if line 1–2 didn't already answer "why me, why now."]
[Ask — one sentence, one action.]
[Your name]

4. Sujan Patel / Mailshake — Data-Backed Short Template

[One sentence: the specific outcome or number relevant to their situation. e.g. "Companies like yours are seeing [X%] improvement in [metric] by doing [specific thing]."]
[One sentence: why you're mentioning it to them specifically — tie to something on their site/profile/role.]
[CTA — soft, e.g. "Worth exploring? Happy to send the data."]
[Your name]
P.S. [optional low-pressure add-on, e.g. a resource link or one extra proof point]

[Bridge — one sentence connecting their past coverage to why your thing is relevant to their audience, not to you.]

[Easy yes/no ask — e.g. "Want me to send the one-pager?"]

7. Katelyn Bourgoin / Lincoln Murphy — Social Proof / Borrowed Credibility
Subject: [Peer company name] + [result], e.g. "How [Peer Co] solved [problem]"
Hi [First Name],
[Open directly with the peer's story — one to two sentences. Let the reader see themselves in it before you mention your own offer at all.]
[One sentence: the mechanism — what actually changed, not just the outcome.]
[One sentence: why you think [First Name]'s company is in a similar spot — must be specific, based on something real (their market, their recent move, their scale).]
[Ask — low-friction, curiosity-based. e.g. "Want me to walk you through how they did it?"]
[Your name]

8. Alex Berman-Style — Direct/Punchy Outreach
Subject: [Blunt, benefit-forward, under 6 words]
[First Name] —
[One sentence: the specific result you can get them, stated plainly and confidently. No hedging.]
[One sentence: brief proof — a number, a name, a timeframe.]
[Ask — direct: "Want me to show you how?" or "15 min this week?"]
[Your name]
Note: this style leans aggressive/confident — works best with a strong, quantifiable proof point behind it. Weak without real numbers to back the confidence.

[One sentence connecting that fact to a real problem or opportunity, stated as an observation.]
[One sentence: what you do, stated in terms of the outcome, not the feature. Max 15 words.]

[Your name]

Fill-in-the-blank cheat sheet (use across any template above)
[Trigger fact]: the single most recent, specific, true thing about them you can find
[Peer proof]: a named comparable company + a real number or outcome (only use if true)
[Problem reframe]: their likely pain, stated as an observation, not a diagnosis
[Ask]: always one sentence, always binary/easy to say yes to
Length target: 50–150 words total, always
